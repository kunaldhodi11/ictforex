PREMIUM ANALYST WALKTHROUGH · GOLD & NAS100

One Complete ICT / SMC Trade Model — Step by Step

Learn to read the full sequence on XAUUSD and NAS100. These are educational chart-reading rules, not claims about hidden institutional orders. A chart can show price behaviour and likely liquidity areas; it cannot identify a bank's actual position or prove who entered a trade.

MARKET 01 · GOLD

XAUUSD — Liquidity Raid to Bearish Shift

1. Context: Start on 1H/15M. Mark the dealing range, recent swing highs/lows, previous day high/low and session range.

2. Liquidity: Mark equal highs and obvious highs as potential buy-side liquidity. Explain why breakout buyers may enter above them and short sellers may place stops there.

3. Sweep: Wait for price to trade above the marked high and then close back below. A wick alone is not enough to call a reversal.

4. MSS: On 5M/1M, identify a decisive break below the relevant internal swing low, preferably with displacement.

5. FVG: Mark the bearish three-candle imbalance created by displacement. Define its upper and lower boundaries precisely.

6. Order Block: Identify the last bullish candle/range before the bearish displacement. Keep it only if it is connected to the structure break and setup context.

7. Entry & invalidation: Wait for retracement and a clear trigger. Stop belongs beyond the technical invalidation/sweep high, not at an arbitrary tight distance.

8. Target: Plan toward a logical sell-side liquidity pool or prior low. If price does not confirm, there is no trade.

MARKET 02 · NASDAQ 100

NAS100 — Sell-Side Sweep to Bullish Shift

1. Context: Read 1H/15M structure first. Select one meaningful dealing range instead of marking every small swing.

2. Liquidity: Mark equal lows, a clear swing low, or previous session low. Explain that long traders' protective stops and breakout sell orders may sit below visible lows.

3. Sweep & reclaim: Price trades below the low, then returns above it. A continued close below may instead be a genuine breakdown.

4. Bullish MSS: Wait for displacement through the last meaningful lower high on the execution timeframe.

5. FVG: Mark the bullish gap between candle 1 high and candle 3 low. Do not label every gap as tradable.

6. Bullish OB: Mark the last bearish candle/range before the displacement leg. Check whether it caused a meaningful break.

7. Entry & risk: Wait for a retracement reaction. Place invalidation below the sweep low/structure; size the position from the stop distance.

8. Target: Use opposing buy-side liquidity or a prior swing high. Record the outcome even when the setup fails.

How to identify traps and institutional activity responsibly

Common trap location: obvious equal highs/lows, prior-day extremes, range boundaries and breakout levels. These areas may attract clustered orders; they do not guarantee a trap.

What we can observe: sweep, reclaim, displacement, structure shift, imbalance and retest. We cannot see hidden institutional orders on a normal candlestick chart or state exactly where an institution entered.

Every lesson must show: timeframe, symbol, context, marked level, candle sequence, confirmation, entry condition, invalidation, target, and at least one failure scenario.

01 · STRUCTURE

HH, HL, LH, LL & BOS

Mark clear swing points, not every tiny fluctuation. In an uptrend, price tends to form Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL). In a downtrend, it tends to form Lower Lows (LL) and Lower Highs (LH).

BOS — Break of Structure: a decisive break of a meaningful swing in the direction of the prevailing move. Prefer a candle close and displacement over a wick-only poke.

02 · POSSIBLE SHIFT

CHoCH / MSS

CHoCH — Change of Character: an early sign that the prior sequence may be changing. MSS — Market Structure Shift: commonly used for a more decisive opposing swing break, ideally after a liquidity event and with displacement.

Terminology varies between SMC traders. Define your own swing rules before backtesting so you do not relabel every small break.

03 · LIQUIDITY MAP

BSL, SSL, EQH & EQL

BSL: Buy-Side Liquidity, often discussed above obvious highs. SSL: Sell-Side Liquidity, often discussed below obvious lows. EQH/EQL: Equal Highs / Equal Lows that make visible reference levels.

Also mark previous day high/low, previous session high/low and range boundaries. These are areas where orders may cluster—not proof of a guaranteed stop hunt.

04 · SWEEP & RECLAIM

Liquidity Sweep / Stop Run

For a bullish idea, price may trade below a clear low (SSL) and then reclaim it. For a bearish idea, price may trade above a clear high (BSL) and then return below it.

A wick alone is not enough. If price accepts beyond the level and continues, it may be a genuine breakout instead of a reversal. Wait for structure confirmation.

05 · IMPULSE

Displacement & Imbalance

Displacement is a strong directional move, often shown by larger candle bodies, less overlap and a meaningful structure break. It can leave an imbalance or Fair Value Gap (FVG).

FVG: in a bullish three-candle pattern, candle 1 high is below candle 3 low; the gap between them is the zone. Reverse the rule for a bearish FVG. Not every gap is tradable or must fill.

06 · POINT OF INTEREST

Order Block, Breaker & Mitigation

OB: the last opposing candle/range before a displacement leg that contributes to a meaningful structure break. Breaker: a failed OB that may act as an opposing-side retest zone after a decisive break.

Mitigation block: a trader-defined reaction area associated with prior price movement. These labels are subjective; use consistent rules and avoid marking every candle as an OB.

07 · LOCATION FILTER

Premium, Discount & Equilibrium

Choose a meaningful dealing range from a swing low to swing high. Its midpoint is Equilibrium (50%). The upper half is Premium; the lower half is Discount.

For a bullish idea, discount may be a more favourable location; for a bearish idea, premium may be. This is only a location filter—not an entry trigger. Combine it with liquidity and confirmation.

08 · ENTRY TRIGGER

NAS100 Bullish SMC Sequence

  1. 1H/15M context is bullish or price reaches a planned higher-timeframe demand area.
  2. Mark SSL below equal lows, a prior low or a session low.
  3. Wait for a sweep and reclaim; do not buy the first wick.
  4. On 5M, wait for bullish displacement and MSS through a meaningful lower high.
  5. Mark the FVG and valid bullish OB created by the displacement.
  6. Wait for a retracement into the chosen POI and a predefined 1M/5M trigger.
09 · COMPLETE EXECUTION PLAN

Entry, Stop Loss, Take Profit & Invalidation

Entry: use a preselected trigger inside the FVG/OB confluence after the sweep and MSS. Do not chase if price leaves without a retracement.

Stop Loss: place it beyond the sweep low for a long or sweep high for a short, with enough room for normal price movement. The stop must represent the point where the setup is invalid—not a distance chosen to force a 1:4 ratio.

Take Profit: first map opposing liquidity, prior swing highs/lows, session extremes and higher-timeframe levels. A 1:4 target is valid only if the chart has enough room to that objective. If the next logical target is only 2R away, do not pretend it is a 4R setup.

Cancel the idea: if price closes through the invalidation level, the MSS fails, the POI is decisively broken, or the target is blocked by nearby structure.

10 · RISK / REWARD EXAMPLE

How a 1:4 Risk-to-Reward Plan Works

R means the amount risked between entry and stop. A 1:4 plan aims to make four times the planned risk if the target is reached, before spread, commission and slippage.

−1R

Entry to invalidation

TARGET REWARD
+4R

BREAK-EVEN WIN RATE
20%

Illustrative price-unit example (not a NAS100 quote): Long entry 100.00, stop 99.00 = 1 unit risk; target 104.00 = 4 units reward. For a short: entry 100.00, stop 101.00, target 96.00. Real NAS100 point value, tick size and contract size depend on the broker.

Position sizing: risk amount = account equity × chosen risk %. Calculate lot size from the stop distance and your broker's tick/contract value. If the stop is wider, reduce size. Never widen the stop to avoid a loss.

Reality check: 1:4 does not mean easy or frequent wins. A 20% win rate is only theoretical break-even before costs if every winner is exactly +4R and every loser is exactly −1R. Track at least 30–50 rule-based examples and include costs, slippage, partial exits and missed fills.

11 · FAILED SETUPS & TRADER TRAPS

When NOT to Enter

  • Price sweeps a low but never reclaims it.
  • There is no clear displacement or meaningful MSS.
  • The FVG/OB is far away and the entry would require chasing.
  • The next opposing liquidity is too close to offer a realistic 4R target.
  • High-impact news, spread expansion or thin liquidity makes execution unreliable.
  • Daily loss limit is reached or the setup risks more than the pre-defined amount.

Record the result in R, including failed setups. Do not publish a 1:4 result unless the chart's actual entry and stop-to-target distances support it.

LESSON 01 · ICT CONCEPTS

ICT Market Structure

Meaning: Identify meaningful swing highs and lows, along with the prevailing directional structure.

Chart identification: Identify the HH/HL or LH/LL sequence; confirm a relevant swing break with a candle close.

Educational plan: Example: after a bullish bias, watch for a swing-high break; invalidation is below the recent HL.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 02 · ICT CONCEPTS

Market Structure Shift (MSS)

Meaning: MSS may indicate a short-term directional change; it is not a guarantee.

Chart identification: Identify a decisive break of the opposing swing and displacement after a liquidity event.

Educational plan: Example: SSL sweep → bullish MSS; enter only after confirmation, with the SL below the sweep low.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 03 · ICT CONCEPTS

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

Meaning: A three-candle imbalance is a non-overlapping zone between candles 1 and 3.

Chart identification: Bullish: the gap is between candle 1's high and candle 3's low; bearish is the reverse.

Educational plan: Study the reaction on an FVG retest; not every gap gets filled.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 04 · ICT CONCEPTS

Bullish FVG

Meaning: A bullish imbalance zone formed by strong upward displacement.

Chart identification: Mark the gap between candle 1's high and candle 3's low.

Educational plan: Consider a potential long only with context and a trigger; place the SL below structural invalidation.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 05 · ICT CONCEPTS

Bearish FVG

Meaning: A bearish imbalance zone formed by strong downward displacement.

Chart identification: Mark the gap between candle 1's low and candle 3's high.

Educational plan: Consider a potential short only with context and a trigger; place the SL above structural invalidation.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 06 · ICT CONCEPTS

Inverse FVG (IFVG)

Meaning: A broken FVG can be studied as an opposing-side reaction zone.

Chart identification: Identify a decisive price move through the FVG; observe a possible role reversal on the retest.

Educational plan: Do not assume a zone has flipped without confirmation; place the stop beyond invalidation.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 07 · ICT CONCEPTS

OTE — Optimal Trade Entry

Meaning: Common ICT Fibonacci retracement framework, often 62–79% band.

Chart identification: Anchor the Fibonacci tool to a meaningful impulse swing; wait for confluence and a trigger.

Educational plan: OTE is a location filter, not an automatic entry; place the SL beyond swing invalidation.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 08 · ICT CONCEPTS

ICT Kill Zones

Meaning: Selected London/New York time windows when market activity may change.

Chart identification: Verify broker/server time and daylight-saving conversion; check spreads and news.

Educational plan: A time window alone does not create a setup; an entry model and risk rules are essential.

LESSON 09 · ICT CONCEPTS

Judas Swing

Meaning: A trading-session narrative in which an initial false move may be followed by expansion in the opposite direction.

Chart identification: Look for a sweep at the range boundary, a return inside the range, and displacement or structural confirmation.

Educational plan: Do not assume a false move or take a counter-trend trade without confirmation.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 10 · ICT CONCEPTS

ICT Entry Model

Meaning: Structured sequence: liquidity event, displacement/MSS, POI retracement, execution.

Chart identification: Higher-timeframe context → sweep → MSS → FVG/OB retest.

Educational plan: Define the entry trigger, invalidation-based stop-loss, and logical liquidity target in advance.

Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)

Meaning: A potential stop or breakout-order area above obvious highs.

Chart identification: Mark equal highs, previous highs, and swing highs.

Educational plan: BSL provides a target or sweep context; a reversal is not guaranteed.

Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)

Meaning: A potential stop or breakout-order area below obvious lows.

Chart identification: Mark equal lows, previous lows, and swing lows.

Educational plan: The trend may continue even after an SSL sweep.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 03 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Equal Highs (EQH)

Meaning: Similar highs that may serve as visible liquidity references for traders.

Chart identification: Mark repeated highs as a zone; exact price equality is not required.

Educational plan: Observe whether price accepts above the level or returns inside the range.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 04 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Equal Lows (EQL)

Meaning: Similar lows that may serve as potential sell-side liquidity references.

Chart identification: Mark repeated lows or the zone, taking the instrument's volatility into account.

Educational plan: A touch of the level is not an automatic buy or sell signal.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 05 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Liquidity Sweep

Meaning: Price trades beyond an obvious level and then returns inside the range.

Chart identification: After the wick or candle close returns inside the range, look for displacement and MSS confirmation.

Educational plan: A wick alone is not an entry signal; the stop may be planned beyond the sweep extreme.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 06 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Liquidity Grab

Meaning: A short-lived move through a visible level; usage of the term varies among traders.

Chart identification: Observe the level breach, quick rejection, and subsequent follow-through.

Educational plan: Clearly define the terms sweep and grab in your trading rules.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 07 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Internal vs External Liquidity

Meaning: Minor pivots inside the range are internal liquidity; levels beyond the range boundaries are external liquidity references.

Chart identification: Define the dealing range; mark internal swing points and the external highs and lows.

Educational plan: Do not define the range arbitrarily; use higher-timeframe context.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 08 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Inducement (IDM)

Meaning: A minor swing or setup that may attract early participants—an interpretive concept.

Chart identification: Observe the local pivot before the main point of interest and the subsequent clearance.

Educational plan: Do not claim manipulation with certainty; use objective rules.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 09 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Liquidity Run

Meaning: Price clears multiple visible liquidity levels in the direction of the trend.

Chart identification: Look for repeated level breaks, price acceptance, and continuation structure.

Educational plan: Avoid taking premature reversal trades against the liquidity run.

Illustrative chart only · No live signal

LESSON 10 · LIQUIDITY CONCEPTS

Liquidity-Based Entry Model

Meaning: Combine a liquidity event with a market-structure shift and point-of-interest confirmation.

Chart identification: Key level → sweep/acceptance → displacement/MSS → retest.

Educational plan: Predefine the entry, technical stop-loss, target, position size, and conditions for not trading.

VISUAL ICT / SMC TRAINING

Chart Analysis Lessons — Learn by Seeing

These annotated charts are educational illustrations, not live prices or trade signals. Follow the sequence to understand how a trader can build a plan. Always confirm the same conditions on the live chart before considering a trade.

Lesson 1 — Gold (XAUUSD): Liquidity Sweep to Bearish Setup

  1. Mark liquidity: identify obvious swing highs/lows and equal highs/lows where stop orders may cluster.
  2. Wait for the sweep: price trades beyond a visible liquidity level, then watch whether it rejects and returns.
  3. Seek confirmation: look for displacement and a market-structure shift; a wick alone is not confirmation.
  4. Refine the area: study the last opposing candle (Order Block) and any Fair Value Gap left by displacement.
  5. Plan risk: define invalidation beyond the sweep/structure and choose a realistic target at opposing liquidity. Position size comes from the stop distance and risk limit.

Practice task: find three historical XAUUSD examples and mark the sweep, confirmation, OB/FVG, invalidation and target. Record examples that fail too.

Lesson 2 — NAS100: Liquidity Sweep to Bullish Setup

  1. Set context: use a higher timeframe to identify direction, range and important swing points.
  2. Mark sell-side liquidity: note equal lows and clear swing lows below price.
  3. Wait for a sweep and reclaim: price takes liquidity and returns above the level; wait for bullish displacement / structure shift.
  4. Identify the retracement zone: mark a valid bullish Order Block or FVG created by displacement.
  5. Define the trade: entry is only considered after confirmation; stop goes beyond the invalidation point and target is chosen from a logical liquidity objective.

Practice task: repeat the process on 5-minute NAS100 charts, then compare with 15-minute and 1-hour context. Do not assume every sweep reverses.
