Module 01 · Price Action

What Is Price Action?

Price action is the study of how price moves on a chart. Traders observe candle opens, highs, lows and closes, together with the sequence of price swings, to understand market behaviour. Start with the chart itself; indicators may support analysis, but should not replace reading price.

Look at three things: overall direction, important turning points, and how price reacts around previous areas. A candle or pattern is never a guarantee of what happens next.

Module 02 · Price Action

Understanding Market Structure

Market structure is the sequence of meaningful swing highs and swing lows. A developing uptrend commonly forms Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL). A developing downtrend commonly forms Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL).

HH = a swing high above the previous relevant high. HL = a swing low above the previous relevant low. LH = a swing high below the previous relevant high. LL = a swing low below the previous relevant low. These labels describe price; they are not automatic entry signals.

Module 03 · Price Action

How to Identify the Trend

Compare several clear swing points rather than judging the last one or two candles. Rising highs and lows suggest an uptrend; falling highs and lows suggest a downtrend. Overlapping swings and repeated movement between similar boundaries suggest a range.

If the chart is unclear, call it uncertain and wait. A trend is not confirmed by one strong candle, and trends can pause or change.

Module 04 · Price Action

Swing Highs and Swing Lows

A swing high is a local peak where price rises and then moves lower. A swing low is a local trough where price falls and then moves higher. Major swings are usually more useful than every small fluctuation.

A turning point becomes clearer only after price moves away from it. While a candle is still forming, its final high, low and close are not yet known. Do not treat every swing as guaranteed support or resistance.

Module 05 · Price Action

Support and Resistance Areas

Support is an area where falling price has previously slowed or bounced. Resistance is an area where rising price has previously slowed or turned down. Treat them as zones rather than perfectly precise lines.

Mark only clear areas with visible past reactions. Price can break either zone, so wait to see how it behaves when it returns. For the full lesson, review Chapter 1: Support & Resistance.

Module 06 · Price Action

Reading Price Movement in Context

Price action analysis is not about memorising candle shapes. It is about understanding where price is moving, what important areas it is approaching, and whether the market is making progress or struggling to continue.

What to observe on the chart

  1. Direction: Is price making higher swing points, lower swing points, or moving sideways?
  2. Location: Is price near a previous swing high or low, a range boundary, or a support/resistance area?
  3. Progress: Does price continue beyond the previous swing, or does it repeatedly fail to move further?
  4. Reaction: After reaching an important area, does price move away, pause, or remain inside the same range?
  5. Confirmation: Wait for the market to show enough evidence for your plan. Do not make a decision from one isolated candle.
Example: if price approaches a previous swing high several times but cannot move beyond it, note that buyers may be struggling at that area. This is an observation, not a guaranteed sell signal. Wait for the broader structure and your trade rules to provide confirmation.

Module 07 · Price Action

Breakouts, Retests and Failed Breakouts

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond a watched range or level. A candle close beyond the area can provide more information than a brief wick through it, but even a close can fail. A retest is a return towards the broken area.

A failed breakout happens when price moves beyond a boundary but then returns inside the previous range. Avoid assuming a breakout has succeeded until your own confirmation conditions are met.

Module 08 · Price Action

Pullbacks vs Possible Reversals

A pullback is a temporary move against the broader direction. In an uptrend, price may fall before attempting to continue higher; in a downtrend, price may rise before sellers return.

A possible reversal is a change in the prevailing direction. Look for meaningful swing levels to fail and a new sequence of highs and lows to develop. One opposite-colour candle does not prove a reversal.

Module 09 · Price Action

Understanding Sideways Markets

In a sideways market, price repeatedly moves between an upper and lower area without a sustained sequence of rising or falling swings. Mark the visible boundaries and observe how price behaves near them.

The middle of a range can offer poor clarity because price is far from either boundary. A range can end suddenly, so do not assume it will continue indefinitely.

Module 10 · Price Action

Using Multiple Timeframes

A higher timeframe helps show the broader direction and important areas. A setup timeframe helps you assess the current structure. A lower timeframe can show more detail for entry and invalidation, but it also contains more noise.

Choose timeframes that suit your holding period and test the combination consistently. There is no single best timeframe combination for every trader.

Module 11 · Price Action

Build a Simple Price Action Trade Plan

First describe the market condition. Next mark a few meaningful swing points and support/resistance areas. Then define what price must do before you consider entering.

Before entry, decide where the idea becomes invalid, where the target may be, and how much you could lose. If the setup does not meet your rules, skip it. Record a screenshot and review whether you followed the plan.

Module 12 · Price Action

Risk Management Basics

Risk management defines how much you could lose if a trade fails. Set a small, predefined risk amount you can afford to lose. Calculate position size from account risk, stop distance and the instrument's contract specifications.

Place a stop at a logical invalidation point and do not move it farther away just to avoid a loss. Set a daily loss limit and avoid revenge trading. Stops may slip in fast markets; leveraged forex trading carries substantial risk.

Module 13 · Price Action

Common Price Action Mistakes

Common mistakes include marking every tiny swing, entering before a candle closes, treating levels as exact lines, chasing large breakout candles, ignoring the broader chart and changing rules after one loss.

Keep the chart readable. Follow a written process and review a meaningful sample of trades before changing a tested approach.

Module 14 · Price Action

Price Action Practice Checklist

Practise identifying the market condition, marking clear swing highs and lows, and noting nearby support and resistance. Then describe what price would need to do to confirm or invalidate a trade idea.

Use historical charts or a demo account first. Record examples of trends, ranges, pullbacks, breakouts and failed breakouts. The goal is to build a repeatable process, not to force a trade.
