The First Trading Manual: Foundation Course and Strategies

Tom Hougaard

A beginner-friendly, 30-topic trading guide covering market structure, price action, chart timeframes, trade planning, order execution, risk management, trading psychology, and performance reviews.

Important: These are original educational study notes created by ICT Forex Academy. They are not the complete book or an official chapter-by-chapter reproduction from the publisher. The 30 topics are organised learning sections for beginner trading education; they should not be treated as the book's exact chapter titles or sequence.     Key concept

    Market Sessions and Daily Preparation

    Liquidity, volatility, and market participation can vary across trading sessions.

    • The forex market remains active across overlapping global sessions during the trading week; exact timings can change with daylight saving time.
    • Activity may increase during the London/New York overlap, but volatility is not the same every day.
    • Start a session only after checking the economic calendar, major news, spreads, and your trading plan.
    Key concept

    Trend and Market Structure

    A basic approach to identifying trends and ranges through price swings.

    • An uptrend shows a sequence of higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend shows lower highs and lower lows.
    • In a range, price oscillates between defined highs and lows; false breaks can occur before a breakout is confirmed.
    • Always specify the timeframe when describing a trend; the H4 trend and M5 structure may differ.
    Key concept

    Price Action and Context

    Read candles in the context of surrounding market structure and location, not as isolated signals.

    • A strong close may indicate momentum, but it does not guarantee continuation in the next candle.
    • Combine support and resistance, recent swing points, volatility, and news context.
    • Do not buy in a falling market simply because the price looks 'cheap'; wait for evidence of a reversal.
    Key concept

    Candlestick Formations

    Candlestick patterns visually summarise buyer-seller interaction; their reliability depends on location and confirmation.

    • A Doji or spinning top may indicate indecision; the pattern alone does not confirm a reversal.
    • Candles such as a hammer or shooting star are meaningful when considered in the context of the trend and a key level.
    • A large-bodied candle may indicate momentum; assess stop distance and reward-to-risk before entering.
    Key concept

    Entry Triggers, Stop Losses, and Trade Management

    A repeatable trading plan should define the setup, trigger, invalidation point, and exit in advance.

    • Before entering, write down the setup, the condition that invalidates the trade, the stop-loss level, and the reasoning behind the target.
    • Place the stop loss according to market structure and volatility; an arbitrary fixed pip distance does not suit every pair.
    • Calculate risk per trade using account size and stop distance; do not increase position size to recover a loss.
    Key concept

    Psychology, Routine, and Review

    Consistency comes from following a process, not from one winning trade or a claimed high win rate.

    • Record the setup, entry, exit, screenshot, risk, and emotional state in a trading journal.
    • Avoid revenge trading after a loss and overconfidence after a win.
    • Evaluate a strategy over a sufficient sample size; consider average wins and losses, drawdown, and costs alongside the win rate.
    Study topic 7 of 30

    Trading plan and written rules

    A trading plan converts setup selection, timing, risk, trade management, and review into written rules.

    • Keep entry and exit conditions objective.
    • Define no-trade conditions as well.
    • Test rules using historical and demo data before risking live capital.
    Study topic 8 of 30

    Choosing a market and instrument

    Each instrument has its own spread, volatility, liquidity, and trading hours.

    • Compare the behaviour of forex pairs, indices, and commodities.
    • Focus on a limited number of instruments at a time.
    • Understand contract size, tick value, and trading costs.
    Study topic 9 of 30

    Timeframes and multi-timeframe context

    A higher timeframe provides broader context, while a lower timeframe can provide execution details.

    • Choose the analysis and execution timeframes in advance.
    • Signals across timeframes can conflict.
    • Filter lower-timeframe noise using the higher-timeframe context.
    Study topic 10 of 30

    Support and resistance zones

    Support and resistance areas are price zones where buying or selling interest has previously been visible.

    • Consider a zone rather than a single exact line.
    • Repeated tests may reinforce a level or lead to its eventual break.
    • A retest may occur after a breakout, but it is not guaranteed.
    Study topic 11 of 30

    Breakouts and false breakouts

    A breakout is a price move beyond a defined range or level; a false breakout may return to the range.

    • Observe the candle close and subsequent follow-through.
    • Consider volume, volatility, and news context.
    • Define the invalidation point rather than chasing a breakout.
    Study topic 12 of 30

    Pullbacks and continuation

    A pullback may be a temporary retracement against the trend; a continuation setup requires confirmation.

    • Assess the depth of the pullback and the market-structure context.
    • Look for evidence that the trend remains intact.
    • Not every retracement leads to trend continuation.
    Study topic 13 of 30

    Momentum and market conditions

    Momentum describes the speed and strength of price movement.

    • Counter-trend trades can be risky during strong momentum.
    • Momentum indicators may lag or produce noisy signals.
    • Treat trending, ranging, and high-volatility conditions differently.
    Study topic 14 of 30

    Volatility and stop distance

    Volatility describes how much price may move; stop distance should be adapted to market conditions.

    • Tools such as ATR can provide context about recent price ranges.
    • A very tight stop may be triggered by normal market noise.
    • Reduce position size when widening the stop distance.
    Study topic 15 of 30

    Position sizing and account risk

    Position size is calculated using account risk, stop distance, and instrument value.

    • Decide the risk amount before entering a trade.
    • Verify pip or tick value and contract specifications.
    • The availability of leverage does not make a trade safe.
    Study topic 16 of 30

    Reward-to-risk and expectancy

    Reward-to-risk compares the planned reward with the planned risk; expectancy estimates the average edge across outcomes.

    • A high reward-to-risk ratio alone does not prove that a system is profitable.
    • Evaluate win rate, average win and loss, and trading costs together.
    • Include a realistic sample size and drawdown.
    Study topic 17 of 30

    Order types and execution

    Market, limit, and stop orders involve different execution trade-offs.

    • A market order prioritises execution speed but does not guarantee a price.
    • A limit order provides price control but does not guarantee execution.
    • Slippage may occur when a stop order is triggered.
    Study topic 18 of 30

    Spread, commission and slippage

    Trading costs can materially affect a strategy's net results.

    • Spreads may widen during certain sessions and around news events.
    • Include commissions and swap or financing charges.
    • Use realistic slippage assumptions in backtests.
    Study topic 19 of 30

    Economic calendar and news risk

    Economic releases can quickly change volatility, spreads, and execution conditions.

    • Identify high-impact events in advance.
    • Understand gap risk from unexpected headlines.
    • Choosing not to trade during news events can also be a valid plan decision.
    Study topic 20 of 30

    Trading sessions and liquidity

    Session overlaps may increase activity, but liquidity and volatility differ by currency pair.

    • Some currency pairs may be more active during London and New York trading hours.
    • Do not automatically assume that the Asian session has low volatility.
    • Verify broker server time and daylight-saving changes.
    Study topic 21 of 30

    Trading journal and statistics

    A journal turns decisions and outcomes into objective evidence.

    • Record the setup, screenshot, risk, exit, and emotions.
    • Track R-multiple, expectancy, and drawdown.
    • Do not draw conclusions about a strategy from a small sample.
    Study topic 22 of 30

    Backtesting and forward testing

    Backtesting checks rules against historical data; forward testing evaluates the process on unseen or live demo data.

    • Do not repeatedly change the rules during testing.
    • Include spreads, commissions, and slippage.
    • Avoid overfitting and look-ahead bias.
    Study topic 23 of 30

    Trading discipline and patience

    Discipline means waiting for valid setups and following established rules.

    • Avoid FOMO entries and impulsive trades.
    • A no-trade day can also be an example of successful discipline.
    • Keep the routine manageable and repeatable.
    Study topic 24 of 30

    Emotional control after losses

    Losses are a normal part of trading; emotional reactions can multiply risk.

    • Consider setting a predefined daily loss limit.
    • Do not increase lot size to recover a loss.
    • Take breaks and review your journal.
    Study topic 25 of 30

    Managing winning trades

    Exit logic is just as important as entry logic when managing a winning trade.

    • Define the target, trailing stop, or structure-based exit in advance.
    • Do not move a stop farther away without a valid reason.
    • Test the costs and effects of partial exits.
    Study topic 26 of 30

    Common beginner mistakes

    Common errors include overtrading, excessive leverage, random indicators, and frequent changes to the plan.

    • Focus on one simple setup.
    • Verify social-media trading signals independently.
    • Do not trust claims of guaranteed profits or fixed win rates.
    Study topic 27 of 30

    Building a repeatable routine

    A pre-market, during-market, and post-market routine helps make execution more consistent.

    • Review the economic calendar and key levels before the session.
    • Use a checklist while trading.
    • Update your journal and screenshots after the session.
    Study topic 28 of 30

    Capital preservation and drawdown

    Capital preservation is essential for long-term participation.

    • Calculate scenarios involving consecutive losses.
    • Define rules for reducing risk during drawdowns.
    • Keep emergency savings separate from trading capital.
    Study topic 29 of 30

    Demo trading and gradual transition

    A demo account is useful for practising a platform and strategy, but live trading emotions and executions can differ.

    • Demonstrate consistent rule-following in a demo account.
    • Use the smallest practical risk when transitioning to live trading.
    • Progress gradually based on performance and comfort.
    Study topic 30 of 30

    Reviewing and improving a strategy

    Strategy improvements should be evidence-based, not driven by random additions of indicators.

    • Identify weaknesses using journal statistics.
    • Test one change at a time.
    • Revalidate assumptions when the market regime changes.
    No trading pattern or setup is guaranteed. Test the book's concepts in current markets, practise with demo or paper trading, and define the risk of every trade in advance.
